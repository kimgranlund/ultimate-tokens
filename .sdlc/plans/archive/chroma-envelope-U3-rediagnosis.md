# U3 re-diagnosis: the tone hold and the per-stop OKHSL hue solve on the anchored path

## The question (R77 Q2)

C3.3 x2 bars every anchored `hueSpace: "oklch"` row at `<= 0.01` L* between `toneTarget` at the shipped `damp` and `toneHeld` at `damp` 0; at U3's head 42,112 of 251,064 rows miss it (max 1.007 L*). `anchor.mjs` f4 bounds the default kit's hueSpace flip at `<= 2` codes per stop; at U3's head it reads 3 (perceptual Warning 150, peak Info 350). The builder's basis-`s` solve clears x2 and takes f4 peak to 11. The owner asked for one construction that meets both, measured, or a proof that none exists within U3's scope.

## Mechanism: the per-stop solve solves an identity and reads the 8-bit staircase; the hold turns that noise into an L* shift

1. **OKHSL hue IS the OKLCH hue, by construction.** OKHSL keeps OKLab's hue angle unchanged and remaps only `s` and `l` (`okhsl.js`). Measured on the float path: `rgbToOklchHue(okhslToRgbFloat(h, s, l))` against `h` over 5,814 in-gamut points reads max 1.1e-13 degrees; through the 8-bit `okhslToRgb` the residual is the rounding alone, `<= 0.011` degrees where the pixel has chroma. The `solveOkhslHue` header comment's premise (`tonal.js:124` to `:126`, "the two disagree on constant hue ... (Abney), worst in the blues (~6°)") does not reproduce; it describes the even path's CAM16 solve (`solveCam16Hue`), where hue spaces really do differ. On the OKHSL paths the function is analytically the identity and its only work is chasing the staircase its own `#657` comment describes.

2. **At a damped pale stop the staircase solve is degenerate.** Default kit Warning, perceptual, stop 150: `env` 0.03, damped `s` 0.029, every hue in a wide band renders the same pale pixel, so the solve returns the "best" of 17 arbitrary candidates: 106 degrees against the anchor's own 68.7 (`diag.mjs`). The hold then evaluates its target at the BASIS `s` (0.97), where 106 and 68.7 are visibly different colours, so `toneTarget` reads 96.13 against 95.12 at `damp` 0: the x2 miss of 1.007 L* is that hue error made visible at full saturation. Nothing in the hold is wrong; it is fed a hue that the emission would never show.

3. **Per hueSpace the target differs, and the cap's discrete polish amplifies it.** `cam16` rows use `hOkSeed = anchor.okhsl.h`; `oklch` rows the staircase hue. Two targets that differ by a few hundredths of an L* feed `capChromaAtHeldTone` whose `refineNearestRgb` polish is hue-blind and discrete, so at a capped stop the two hueSpaces land on different 8-bit neighbours: Info 350 (`#53A1D2` / `#55A1CF`, 3 codes), Data 6 450, Secondary 450 (11 codes under the basis-`s` solve). The uncapped rows show the same thing without the polish (Warning 150, 3 codes).

4. **Why the two candidates on the table cannot both clear.** The damped-`s` solve gives each hueSpace a different, arbitrary hue at pale stops (x2 fails). The basis-`s` solve gives a hue that is right for the target but is then emitted at the damped `s`, where the staircase makes it land elsewhere; at capped stops the polish diverges by up to 11 codes (f4 fails). Any construction that keeps a per-stop staircase solve keeps one of the two errors.

5. **What removes both.** `anchor.okhsl.h === rgbToOklchHue(anchor.rgb)` exactly (item 1), so the anchored `oklch` and `cam16` paths are asking for the same hue. Using the anchor's own OKLCH hue directly on the `oklch` path (no per-stop solve) makes the two hueSpaces byte-identical on every anchored perceptual and peak row, which is what Q-D ruled (hueSpace has no visible effect there; the UI disables the control, (hs1) to (hs4)) and what the pre-R3 construction measured ("0 of 3,396 moved", `tonal.js:1261`). The per-stop solve added at R3 was a fix for a control that the later ruling accepts as dead on this path.

### Ranking under R78

R78 (owner, 2026-09-30): no per-hue hard-coding; keep the engine simple, honest and flexible; prefer one general construction and one stated tolerance over hue- or key-specific constants. ## Recommended construction: Aid

On `okhslStopsAnchored`'s `hueSpace: "oklch"` branch the emitted OKHSL hue is the anchor's own OKLCH hue, `targetOklchHue` (`rgbToOklchHue(anchor.rgb)`, or `palette.hue` for an achromatic anchor, unchanged), with no per-stop `solveOkhslHue`; the `cam16` branch keeps `hOkSeed`. The two values are equal to floating precision, so both hueSpaces render byte-identical anchored perceptual and peak ramps, and the hold receives the one hue the emission shows. `solveOkhslHue` itself, its `#657` staircase tracking, and the non-anchored path's single solve at stop 500 (`tonal.js:1413`, x2 already 0 there) are untouched. The header comment at `tonal.js:1258` to `:1265` and the inline comment at `:1330` to `:1332` are rewritten to say the solve is the identity on this path and why it is gone (item 5); the `solveOkhslHue` header's Abney sentence is scoped to the even path in the same hunk. It removes 17 `okhslToRgb` renders per anchored oklch stop, which helps C3.7's corpus-anchor row.
