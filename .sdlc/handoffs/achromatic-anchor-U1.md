## Why two allow-lists needed a mechanical re-freeze

Only 2 corpus anchors sit under `ACHROMATIC_ANCHOR_C` (U1-4): Nike tertiary-muted's `#FFFFFF` (hue 0, the generator's hueless-sample fallback) and travel secondary-muted's `#ACADAE`. Both now render at their palette's own hue instead of their rounding-residue one, which moves `maxChromaInGamut`/`anchorChromaBasis`'s per-stop blend at nearby stops even though the pivot chroma itself is unmoved:
- `#FFFFFF`'s ramp at hue 0 collides two adjacent near-white 25-stop peak-mode pairs to the same 8-bit hex (`KNOWN_BASELINE_DUP`, the same rounding-collision class every other member of that list already names).
- `#ACADAE`'s ramp newly notches at its own pivot in peak and perceptual (`NOTCH_ALLOW`, `DIP_BASELINE`/`PERCEPTUAL_DIP_BASELINE`) — the identical `anchorChromaBasis`-vs-`maxChromaInGamut` mechanism `EVEN_DIP_BASELINE`'s own header comment already describes for 32 of its 90 instances, landing on the other two modes this time.

Every other allow-list entry in `test/engine/{anchor,prime,tonal}.mjs` (`ORDER_ALLOW`, `RAMP_WINDOW_ALLOW`, `RAMP_GAP_ALLOW`, `RAMP_DISTINCT_ALLOW`, the two Nike lines in `prime.mjs`) is unchanged — Nike tertiary-muted keeps tripping each of them exactly as before.
