- TOL. The plan rules this from a plan-time probe and does not guess it. Name it once, as exports of `scripts/lib/envelope-measure.mjs`:
  - Path unit: `rgbToOkhsl(rgb).s` on perceptual and peak (`src/engine/okhsl.js:203`). On even, `cam16FromRgb(rgb).chroma` (`src/engine/hct.js:233`), except that an achromatic pixel (r = g = b) reads 0. This is the white-point rule: under the engine's viewing conditions CAM16 reads #FFFFFF at C 2.869.
  - A stop is within TOL when its readback is within 1e-9 of `record.model`, or when `model` lies in `[lo, hi]`, the range of the unit over the emitted pixel's cube of plus or minus K codes per channel (channels clamped to 0..255).
  - `TOL_CODES = 1` (8-bit rounding) for plain stops. `TOL_CODES_TWICE = 2` for stops with `damper < 1` (`dampStops` rounds twice) and for `capped` stops, where the check is one-sided, `lo <= model` only (`refineNearestRgb`). `TOL_CODES_REFINED = 4` (1 plus `enforceMonotonePixelL`'s RADIUS 3) for `refined` stops. Class precedence: refined, then capped, then damped, then plain.
  - Gamut edge (the clipping named once): on the OKHSL path, a pixel with a channel at 0 or 255 whose readback s is at least `EDGE_S = 0.999` takes `hi = Math.max(hi, 1)`. s saturates at the sRGB boundary, and no 8-bit pixel reads exactly 1.
  - Evidence: a scratch probe with exactly these rules, run over 2,920 instances x 3 modes x 25 stops x both paths, found 0 stops outside TOL in all 29 class rows. Without the white-point rule, 2,904 stop-50 #FFFFFF cells fail (C 2.869 against model 0). Without the edge rule, 2,721 saturated stop-500 cells fail (excess at most 8.85e-5 s). A linearized half-code bound fails at grey pixels, which is why the plan uses a cube range.
